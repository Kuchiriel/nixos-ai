# Tea Tree Oil

**Também chamada:** Melaleuca alternifolia

**Condições:** fungal-infection, acne, wounds-cuts, insect-bites-stings, burns

## Uso

Tea tree oil is marketed for external use including acne, athlete's foot, toenail fungus, and lice. Traditional Aboriginal uses included treating wounds, burns, and insect bites.

## Como usar

Apply tea tree oil topically to the affected skin area only — for acne or athlete's foot, use a product containing tea tree oil as directed on the label. Never swallow tea tree oil; oral ingestion can cause serious symptoms including confusion, unsteadiness, and coma.

## Evidência

A small amount of research suggests tea tree oil might be helpful for acne and athlete's foot (fungal infection), though more studies are needed. Evidence for toenail fungus, lice, eyelid inflammation, and oral health uses is insufficient or uncertain. Traditional wound, burn, and insect bite uses lack rigorous clinical research.

## Cautelas

Critical warning: tea tree oil must not be swallowed — oral ingestion can cause serious symptoms including confusion, unsteadiness, inability to walk, and coma. Topical use is generally safe for most adults, though some experience skin redness or irritation. Older products or those exposed to heat, light, or air may increase reaction risk. Products appear safe during pregnancy and breastfeeding when used topically only.
